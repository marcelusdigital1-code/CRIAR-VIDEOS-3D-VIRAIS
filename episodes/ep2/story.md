# Episode 2: The White Gold — Salt

Idea check (2026-11-19): search YouTube for "salt history Short" — best Short ~800K views ("Salt was once worth its weight in gold"). Open — the angle told here (the Roman Via Salaria + tax monopoly + modern irony) is different. No direct overlap found.

## Script (~60 s, Ricks Clips style — paradox → escalation → "You see…" → twist)

The most valuable thing in your kitchen right now is probably salt.
Not your phone. Not your coffee machine.
Salt.
You could be starving — meat, fish, everything rotting — and salt would stop it.
For thousands of years, that made it worth more than gold in some places.
You see, the problem wasn't finding salt. It was moving it.
Merchants dragged it across deserts, over mountains, through routes that could kill you.
In Rome, one of those roads was called the Via Salaria — the Salt Road.
And whoever controlled a salt road controlled who ate and who didn't.
So rulers did what rulers always do.
They taxed it.
In some kingdoms, the salt tax was so high that people risked their lives to smuggle it.
In France, it was called the gabelle — and it helped start a revolution.
Empires were funded by salt. Armies were fed by salt. Cities grew around salt.
The word "salary" comes from the Latin for salt.
Because Roman soldiers were sometimes paid in it.
And the next time you sprinkle it on your food without thinking twice —
that's what power used to look like.
